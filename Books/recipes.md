Cacio e Pepe
Three ingredients, one of Rome's greatest pasta dishes. All about technique.

Servings

1

Ingredients
3.6 ounces tagliatelle
1.1 ounces pecorino, finely grated
0.7 ounces parmesan, finely grated
1.5 teaspoons black pepper, coarsely ground
0.4 cups pasta water (reserved)
1 teaspoons salt
Steps
1
Toast the pepper: Add 1.5 teaspoons black pepper, coarsely ground to a dry pan on medium heat. Toast for 1 minute 
01:00
 until fragrant. Set aside half for the sauce, leave half in the pan.
2
Boil the pasta: Bring a pot of water to the boil, salt lightly (less than usual — the cheese is salty). Cook 3.6 ounces tagliatelle until just under al dente. Reserve at least 0.4 cups pasta water (reserved) before draining.
3
Make the cheese paste: While the pasta cooks, mix 1.1 ounces pecorino, finely grated and 0.7 ounces parmesan, finely grated in a bowl. Add 3–4 tbsp of the hot pasta water and stir into a smooth, creamy paste. It should be thick but pourable.
4
Combine: Add the drained pasta to the pan with the toasted pepper. Add a splash of pasta water and toss. Remove the pan from the heat entirely — this is critical. Add the cheese paste and toss vigorously, adding more pasta water a splash at a time until you have a silky, glossy sauce. It should coat every strand.
5
Serve: Plate immediately and top with the reserved cracked pepper and an extra grating of pecorino.
Notes
The pan must be off the heat when you add the cheese, or it will scramble. Finely grating the cheese (use the small side of the grater) is what makes the paste smooth.

Carbonara
The real Roman carbonara — no cream, ever. Rich, silky, and ready in 20 minutes.

Servings

1

Ingredients
3.6 ounces tagliatelle
2.9 ounces guanciale or pancetta, diced
2 egg yolk
0.9 ounces pecorino, finely grated
0.5 ounces parmesan, finely grated
1 teaspoons black pepper, coarsely ground
0.3 cups pasta water (reserved)
Steps
1
Render the guanciale: Put 2.9 ounces guanciale or pancetta, diced in a cold pan and bring to medium heat. Let it render slowly in its own fat for 8–9 minutes 
09:00
 until golden and crispy at the edges. Remove from heat and set pan aside — you want it warm, not screaming hot.
2
Make the egg mixture: Whisk 2 egg yolk with 0.9 ounces pecorino, finely grated and 0.5 ounces parmesan, finely grated and 1 teaspoons black pepper, coarsely ground in a bowl until combined. It will be thick — that’s fine.
3
Cook the pasta: Boil 3.6 ounces tagliatelle in well-salted water until al dente. Reserve 0.3 cups pasta water (reserved) before draining.
4
Combine off the heat: Add the drained pasta to the pan with the guanciale. Toss. Let the pan cool for 30 seconds — this is crucial. Add the egg mixture and toss quickly, adding pasta water a splash at a time until the sauce is smooth, glossy, and clings to the pasta. If it looks scrambled, the pan was too hot.
5
Serve: Plate immediately, top with any remaining guanciale from the pan, extra pecorino, and a generous crack of black pepper.
Notes
Two yolks for one portion gives a richer sauce than using a whole egg. Guanciale (cured pork cheek) is traditional and worth finding — it has more flavour than pancetta and renders beautifully.

Caesar-ish Salad
A proper Caesar-ish dressing — no bottled stuff, made in minutes in a bowl.

Servings

1

Ingredients
1 pieces romaine lettuce, roughly torn (half a head)

Steps
1
Make the croutons: Toss 2 pieces bread slices (baguette or sourdough), torn into chunks with half of 2 tablespoons olive oil and a pinch of salt. Spread on a baking tray and bake at 200°C for 8–9 minutes 
09:00
 until golden and crisp. Or fry in a pan on medium-high for 4–5 minutes, tossing regularly.
2

Add 1 egg yolk, 1 teaspoons lemon juice, 0.5 teaspoons Dijon mustard, and 0.5 teaspoons Worcestershire sauce. ## 1. Cacio e Pepe
 
*Three ingredients, one of Rome's greatest pasta dishes. All about technique.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~20 mins
 
### Ingredients
- 100g tagliatelle
- 30g pecorino, finely grated
- 20g parmesan, finely grated
- 1½ tsp black pepper, coarsely ground
- 100ml pasta water (reserved)
- 1 tsp salt
 
### Before you start
- Grate both cheeses finely (small side of the grater) before you do anything else
- Have a bowl ready for the cheese paste and a mug ready to scoop pasta water
 
### Method
1. **Toast the pepper** — Add pepper to a dry pan on medium heat. Toast for 60 seconds until fragrant. Set aside half, leave half in the pan.
2. **Boil the pasta** — Use about 2 litres of water in a medium-large pot. Wait for a proper rolling boil (big bubbles, not little ones) before adding the pasta. Salt the water generously — it should taste noticeably salty, like mild seawater. Cook tagliatelle until just under al dente (about 7–8 mins, taste at 7).
3. **Make the cheese paste** — Mix pecorino and parmesan in a bowl. Add 3–4 tbsp of hot pasta water and stir into a smooth, creamy paste. It should be thick but just about pourable. If it's rock solid, add a tiny splash more water.
4. **Scoop pasta water** — Before you drain, scoop out at least a mugful of pasta water. Don't skip this.
5. **Combine** — Add drained pasta to the pan with toasted pepper. Add a splash of pasta water and toss. **Remove the pan from the heat entirely — this is critical.** Add the cheese paste and toss vigorously, adding more pasta water a splash at a time until you have a silky, glossy sauce. Don't oil the pasta after draining.
6. **Serve** — Plate immediately, top with the reserved cracked pepper and extra pecorino.
 
> **Tip:** The pan must be off the heat when you add the cheese, or it will scramble. If the sauce looks too thick, add pasta water. If it looks grainy, the pan was too hot.

## 2. Carbonara
 
*The real Roman carbonara — no cream, ever. Rich, silky, and ready in 20 minutes.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~20 mins
 
### Ingredients
- 100g tagliatelle
- 80g guanciale or pancetta, diced
- 2 egg yolks
- 25g pecorino, finely grated
- 15g parmesan, finely grated
- 1 tsp black pepper, coarsely ground
- 80ml pasta water (reserved)
 
### Before you start
- Separate your egg yolks into a bowl and discard the whites
- Grate both cheeses finely and mix them into the yolks with the pepper — it will be very thick, that's right
- Dice the guanciale or pancetta if it isn't already
 
### Method
1. **Render the guanciale first** — Put guanciale in a *cold* pan and bring to medium heat slowly. Don't preheat the pan. This renders the fat out properly. It needs 8–10 minutes, so start this before your pasta water even boils. You want it golden and crispy at the edges.
2. **Get your pasta water on** — Use about 2 litres of water in a large pot (even a 4 litre pot with 2 litres of water in it is fine — bigger is better). Wait for a proper rolling boil (big bubbles) before adding the pasta. Salt it generously — it should taste like mild seawater.
3. **Turn down or remove the pancetta** — If your pasta water is taking a while, turn the pancetta right down or off. You just need it to stay warm. A cooler pan is actually better for the combining step.
4. **Cook the pasta** — Drop the tagliatelle into the boiling water, separate the strands as you add it, and stir immediately. Cook for about 8 minutes. Taste at 7 — you want it just slightly underdone (a tiny bit of resistance when you bite it, no white chalky centre). It finishes cooking in the pan.
5. **Scoop pasta water** — About a minute before draining, scoop out a good mugful. Don't skip this.
6. **Loosen the egg mixture** — If your cheese paste feels very stiff, add just a tiny splash of pasta water and stir to loosen it slightly. It should be thick but just about pourable.
7. **Combine off the heat** — Add drained pasta to the pan with guanciale. Toss in the fat for 30 seconds. **Take the pan off the heat entirely.** Wait 30 seconds for it to cool. Add the egg and cheese mixture and toss quickly, adding pasta water a splash at a time until the sauce is smooth, glossy, and clings to every strand. If it looks scrambled, the pan was too hot. Don't oil the pasta after draining.
8. **Serve immediately** — Carbonara doesn't keep or reheat well. Eat it straight away.
 
> **Tip:** Two yolks for one portion gives a richer sauce than using a whole egg. Guanciale (cured pork cheek) is traditional and worth finding — it has more flavour than pancetta.

## 3. Aglio e Olio
 
*Garlic, olive oil, chilli, parsley. Barely 15 minutes, barely any effort.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~15 mins
 
### Ingredients
- 100g spaghetti
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 3 tbsp good olive oil
- ½ tsp chilli flakes
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, roughly chopped
- 60ml pasta water (reserved)
- 1 tsp salt
 
### Method
1. **Cook the pasta** — Use about 2 litres of water. Wait for a rolling boil before adding the pasta. Salt generously. Cook spaghetti until al dente (~8 mins). Scoop out a mugful of pasta water before draining.
2. **Infuse the oil** — While the pasta cooks, warm olive oil in a pan on medium-low. Add garlic and chilli flakes. Cook gently for 3–4 minutes, stirring — you want it pale golden. The difference between golden and burnt is about 20 seconds so watch it carefully.
3. **Bring it together** — Add drained pasta to the pan with a good splash of pasta water. Toss over medium heat for 1 minute until the oil and water emulsify into a light sauce. Don't oil the pasta after draining.
4. **Serve** — Remove from heat, add parsley and toss once more. Finish with a tiny drizzle of raw olive oil.
 
> **Tip:** Use your best olive oil here — it is the sauce.

## 4. Tuna Pasta
 
*A proper fridge-raid pasta. Salty, savoury, and done in 20 minutes.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~20 mins  
*Doubles easily for 2 — just double all quantities*
 
### Ingredients
- 100g penne
- 145g tinned tuna in olive oil (see tip if yours is in brine)
- 200g tinned chopped tomatoes
- 2 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1 tbsp capers
- 8 black olives, halved (or chopped finely if you want a milder flavour)
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- ¼ tsp chilli flakes (optional)
- 1 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
 
### Method
1. **Start the sauce first** — Warm olive oil in a pan on medium. Add garlic and chilli flakes and fry for 1 minute until softened but not browned. Add the whole tin of chopped tomatoes, capers, and olives. Stir and turn down to medium-low. Keep the lid off so it can reduce.
2. **Get your pasta water on** — Use about 2 litres. Wait for a rolling boil, salt generously, then add the penne.
3. **Simmer the sauce** — The sauce will look watery at first — that's normal. Keep it bubbling gently for 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally. It will thicken and darken as the water evaporates.
4. **Cook the pasta** — About 10 minutes. Taste at 9. Scoop out a mugful of pasta water before draining.
5. **Combine** — Tip the drained pasta straight into the sauce pan (don't oil it). Toss together. If it needs loosening, add splashes of pasta water.
6. **Finish** — Take the pan off the heat. Flake in the tuna and fold in gently — you want big chunks, not mush. Taste for seasoning.
7. **Serve** — Scatter with parsley. No cheese — tuna and parmesan don't mix.
 
> **If your tuna is in brine:** drain the brine completely (don't add it — it'll make the sauce watery and too salty) and add an extra splash of olive oil to compensate.
 
> **On olives:** whole olives are fine, but if you find them too strong, chop them finely so the flavour spreads through the sauce without big briny chunks. Or leave them out entirely.
 
> **Keeps well:** unlike carbonara, this refrigerates fine for up to 2 days. Add a splash of water when reheating as the pasta will have absorbed a lot of the sauce.

## 5. Arrabiata
 
*Fiery, simple, and better than any jarred arrabiata sauce.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~20 mins
 
### Ingredients
- 100g penne
- 200g tinned chopped tomatoes
- 3 garlic cloves, sliced
- ¾ tsp chilli flakes
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 tbsp fresh parsley or basil, to serve
 
### Method
1. **Fry the garlic and chilli** — Warm olive oil in a pan on medium. Add garlic and chilli flakes. Fry gently for 2 minutes, stirring, until the garlic softens but doesn't colour. Don't rush this step — it's where the flavour comes from.
2. **Simmer the sauce** — Add the whole tin of chopped tomatoes and salt. Stir well. Simmer on medium-low for 12–15 minutes with the lid off, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and darkens slightly.
3. **Cook the pasta** — While the sauce simmers, get 2 litres of water to a rolling boil. Salt generously. Cook penne ~10 mins. Scoop out a mugful of pasta water before draining.
4. **Combine and serve** — Add the pasta straight to the sauce and toss over medium heat for 1 minute, adding a splash of pasta water if needed. Plate and top with parsley or basil.
 
> **Tip:** Adjust chilli to your heat preference — ¾ tsp is a good kick but not overwhelming.

## 7. Caesar-ish Salad
 
*A proper Caesar dressing — no bottled stuff, made in minutes in a bowl.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~15 mins
 
### Ingredients
- ½ head romaine lettuce, roughly torn
- 20g parmesan, shaved or grated
- 2 slices baguette or sourdough, torn into chunks
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 egg yolk
- 2 anchovy fillets
- ½ garlic clove
- 1 tsp lemon juice
- ½ tsp Dijon mustard
- ½ tsp Worcestershire sauce
- Black pepper, to taste
 
### Method
1. **Make the croutons** — Toss bread chunks with half the olive oil and a pinch of salt. Bake at 200°C for 8–10 minutes until golden and crisp, or fry in a pan on medium-high for 4–5 minutes, tossing regularly.

## 8. Pasta Fredda with Crispy Pancetta & Pesto

### Ingredients

## 9. Teriyaki Sauce
 
*A proper from-scratch teriyaki sauce — sweet, glossy, and miles better than the bottled stuff.*
 
**Makes:** ~150ml (enough for 1–2 servings) | **Time:** ~10 mins
 
### Ingredients
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 2 tbsp sake (or dry sherry, or water in a pinch)
- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 garlic clove, grated
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
- 1 tsp cornflour mixed with 1 tbsp cold water (to thicken)
 
### Method
1. **Combine the base** — In a small saucepan, whisk together soy sauce, mirin, sake, and brown sugar.
2. **Add aromatics** — Stir in the grated garlic and ginger.
3. **Simmer** — Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat and cook for 3–4 minutes.
4. **Thicken** — Give the cornflour slurry a quick stir (it settles) and pour it in while whisking. Simmer for another 1–2 minutes until glossy and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
5. **Cool slightly** — Remove from the heat. It will thicken further as it cools.
 
> **Tip:** Keeps in the fridge for up to a week in a sealed jar. This batch makes enough for the teriyaki chicken and rice recipe below, with a little extra for drizzling.

## 10. Teriyaki Chicken and Rice
 
*Sticky, glossy chicken thighs over steamed rice — a proper midweek dinner in 30 minutes.*
 
**Serves:** 1 | **Time:** ~30 mins
 
### Ingredients
- 200g chicken thighs, boneless and skinless, cut into bite-sized pieces
- 1 tbsp vegetable or sunflower oil
- 75g jasmine or short-grain rice
- 1 batch teriyaki sauce (see recipe above)

## General tips that apply to all pasta dishes
 
- **Always wait for a rolling boil** before adding pasta — big bubbles, not little ones. Starting pasta in cold or barely boiling water makes it gluey.
- **Always salt the water generously** — it should taste like mild seawater. This is the only chance you get to season the pasta itself.
- **Use about 2 litres of water** for a single portion (100g pasta). It feels like a lot but pasta needs the room. More is fine, less makes pasta sticky. The size of the pot doesn't matter as long as it holds the water comfortably.
- **Always scoop out a mugful of pasta water** before draining — it's starchy and helps sauces come together. Do it before you drain, not after.
- **Never oil pasta after draining** if you're mixing it into a sauce — it stops the sauce sticking to it.
- **A clove of garlic** is one of the individual segments from a bulb. Break the bulb apart and each small piece is one clove. Peel off the papery skin before using.
- **Which dishes keep in the fridge:** tuna pasta and pesto pasta salad keep well for 1–2 days. Carbonara and cacio e pepe do not — eat them immediately.
